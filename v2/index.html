Product and engineering operating models · AI-native delivery

Becoming AI-Native Is an Operating-Model Change. Not a Tooling Rollout.

Every generation of tooling removes one constraint and exposes the next. Generation is now cheap. Deciding, verifying, releasing and proving value are not. We rebuild product, engineering and the delivery process around where the constraint actually sits.

Read the Thesis

Buying the tools and changing nothing takes two units off a twenty-four unit process.

The Problem

AI Adoption Is Rising. The Bottleneck Is Not Where You Think.

01

Rising Output, Flat Lead Time

Engineers generate more code. The time from intent to production has not changed. Generation filled the build stage; verify and release are now the queue.

02

Unattributable Spend

AI tooling costs are rising. The line to a verified business outcome is not closing. Engineering spend is increasing without a corresponding unit of measured value.

03

Quality Drift

Code churn, revert rates and security defects rise in teams using generation tooling without a review policy. More output does not mean better output.

04

Governance Cost

Compliance, audit and release processes were designed for human-paced delivery. AI-paced output has made them proportionally more expensive per unit shipped.

What Follows from the Thesis

The Constraint Has Moved

If generation is no longer the expensive step, then the cost of building the wrong thing, reviewing it poorly and releasing without evidence is proportionally higher than before. Organisations that optimise for output without improving how they decide and verify will get more of the wrong thing, faster.

What It Predicts

Quality Decay Before Efficiency Gains

Teams that adopt generation tooling without changing how work is specified, checked and judged will first see output rise, then defect rate rise, then lead time rise as the review and release stages saturate. The signal is a growing gap between work started and work that reaches production.

What Would Kill It (Pre-registered)

The Falsifier

If at week twelve the change-failure rate has not fallen and lead time has not shortened for verified features, the approach did not work and we will say so in writing. We treat this as a pre-registered hypothesis, not a success story waiting to be told.

The Method

Four Changes to How Work Moves

01

How Work Starts

Every item requires a committed intent file before any code is generated. The intent names what would make the work unnecessary and what a failing test looks like. No intent, no generation pass.

02

How Work Is Specified

The spec is machine-readable and agreed before generation begins. It binds the generated output to a testable claim. Disputes are resolved against the spec, not against memory of a conversation.

03

How Work Is Checked

Review is policy, not opinion. Every check is run against the spec, recorded and re-runnable. Approval is a gate that produces a record. A conversation that produces no record did not happen.

04

How Work Is Judged

The resolution artefact closes the loop: did this item do what the intent claimed? The answer is required before the next item of the same type can begin. The record calibrates future cycles.

The Artefacts

Six Committed Files. Six Replaced Habits.

01

Intent

Replaces the verbal brief. A named outcome, a falsifier and a statement of what would make this work unnecessary.

02

Spec

Replaces the story ticket. Machine-readable requirements agreed before generation begins. Binds output to a testable claim.

03

Plan

Replaces the task list. A structured generation sequence derived from the spec, with checkpoints and scope limits built in.

04

Change and Tests

Replaces the PR without context. The generated change accompanied by its tests, linked to the spec claim it satisfies.

05

Review Record

Replaces the approval comment. A structured check against the spec, recorded and re-runnable. Approval is a gate, not a message.

06

Resolution

Nothing replaces this — it does not exist yet. A post-release record of whether the intent was achieved, used to calibrate the next cycle.

Evidence

Graded by Method, Not by Vendor

METR
Independent capability evaluations of frontier models on software engineering tasks. Pre-registered methodology, adversarially validated results. Highest methodological weight.
Weight
DORA
Annual State of DevOps Report. Multi-year longitudinal survey of delivery performance across thousands of teams. Broadly representative but self-selected sample.
Weight
GitClear
Analysis of 211 million lines of code change from 2019 to 2024, tracking churn, copy-paste rate and revert frequency. Longitudinal, code-level signal without self-report bias.
Weight
Veracode
Security defect analysis across AI-generated versus human-generated code. Shows higher defect incidence in AI-generated code without an enforced review policy.
Weight
Adoption research
Independent studies of AI coding tool adoption at scale across GitHub Copilot, Cursor and Claude Code. Velocity gains are real; defect-rate outcomes depend on review practice.
Weight
Vendor accounts
Self-reported outcomes from tool providers. Useful for directional signal; structurally incentivised toward positive framing. Treated as hypothesis, not evidence.
Weight

Our own approach would score a C on the same table: we have structured observation and pilot data, not a randomised controlled trial. We state this in advance rather than after the fact.

What Moves

Four Numbers in Four Quarters

↓
Engineering Cost per Unit Shipped
Total engineering spend divided by verified features released to production. Falls as specification quality improves and rework drops.
↓
Change Failure Rate
The proportion of releases requiring a hotfix, rollback or emergency patch. Falls as the review record becomes mandatory and re-runnable rather than conversational.
↓
Contractor and Agency Spend
External capacity bought to compensate for slow or unreliable internal delivery. Reduces as throughput becomes predictable and each unit of work is attributed to a verified outcome.
↓
Time to First Value on Onboarding
Days from engineer hire to first verified contribution in production. Shortens as artefacts replace institutional knowledge held only in people's heads.

When the same method runs more than once across quarters, business units or a portfolio, the resolution artefacts from the first run become the calibration set for the next. The approach compounds in a way a single engagement cannot.

The Programme

Twelve Weeks. Three Phases. Three Gates That Can Fail.

Weeks 1–4

Make the Agent Reliable

Baseline the current delivery flow. Introduce intent and spec artefacts on one representative workstream. Run generation under review policy. Measure defect rate before and after.

Gate: defect rate no higher than baseline. If it is, the programme stops.
Weeks 5–8

Make Review Keep Up

Extend the review record to the full workstream. Automate spec-compliance checks. Reduce review-queue depth below the lead time of a single item. Measure review cycle time.

Gate: review cycle time below 24 hours for 80% of items. If not, scope is cut.
Weeks 9–12

Move the Front of the Process

Introduce the resolution artefact. Rebuild how work starts: intent, spec and plan required before generation on all new items. Measure lead time from intent to verified release.

Gate: week-twelve lead time below week-one baseline. If not, findings are published as a failure.
How We Work

Four Commitments

01

Evidence Graded by Method

We cite our sources, grade them by methodological strength and acknowledge our own position on the same table. No claim lands without a rated source behind it.

02

Enforcement over Guidance

Every recommendation either becomes a gate in the delivery process or it does not count. Guidance that is not enforced is decoration.

03

Artefacts over Ceremony

The six artefacts replace meetings that exist only to share information that should be written down. Less ceremony, more record, same accountability.

04

Falsifiable by Design

We state in advance what would mean the approach did not work. The answer is published regardless of the result.

What We Commit to Saying Did Not Work

  • Change failure rate is unchanged or higher at week twelve.
  • Lead time from intent to verified release has not fallen.
  • Engineering cost per unit shipped has not moved by week sixteen.
  • The team cannot run the review record without us present.
Organisations We Have Worked With

Three Questions Worth Asking

  • What is our current change failure rate, and what does it cost us per quarter?
  • How long does it take from an intent being stated to a verified feature reaching production?
  • If we doubled generation speed today, what stage would saturate first?

If you do not have fast answers to all three, that is the starting point.
