Product and Engineering Operating Models · AI Transformation

Becoming AI-Native Is an Operating-Model Change. Not a Tooling Rollout.

Most businesses have adopted AI tooling. Far fewer have changed how they decide, build and measure. The constraint has moved — and the gap between those two things is where value is being lost.

Read the Thesis

Buying the tools and changing nothing takes two units off a twenty-four unit process.

The Problem

The Tools Have Changed. The Operating Model Hasn't.

01

Operating Model Lag

AI tooling is running on the same decision structures, accountability gaps and measurement approaches that existed before it. The bottleneck has moved — most organisations have not.

02

Spend Without Attribution

AI investment is rising across the portfolio. The line from that spend to a measurable business outcome — margin, delivery efficiency, exit multiple — remains undrawn.

03

Products Not Designed for an AI-Native World

Most roadmaps bolt AI onto what already exists. The harder question is whether the product — what it costs to run, what it can do, how defensible it is — has been redesigned around what AI now makes viable.

04

A Diligence Gap

A buyer asking about AI is not asking about tool adoption. They want to know whether the operating model is more efficient, more defensible and more scalable than the benchmark. Most portcos cannot answer.

What AI-Native Actually Means

A Different Operating Model. Not a Faster Version of the Same One.

Product

Products That Command a Premium

AI-native products do things that weren't previously viable, at economics that weren't previously achievable. Defensibility is structural — built into the business model, not added as a feature layer.

Operating Model

An Operating Model Built for Evidence

How decisions get made, how work flows and how outcomes get measured all need to change together. Speed comes from removing structural friction — not from asking people to move faster on a broken process.

Value at Exit

A Value Story That Holds in Diligence

The evidence of what changed — in cost, speed and reliability — needs to live in the operating data before anyone asks for it. Built-in before the process starts, not constructed during it.

What Moves

Four Numbers. Four Quarters.

↓
Cost to Deliver a Verified Business Outcome
Total engineering and delivery spend against outputs that reached production and can be attributed to a business result. Falls as decision quality improves and rework drops.
↓
Time from Decision to Measurable Result
The elapsed time between a priority being stated and its impact being visible in operating data. Falls when the operating model is rebuilt around where the constraint actually sits.
↓
Dependency on External Capacity
Agency, contractor and consultant spend bought to compensate for capability and capacity gaps. Falls as throughput becomes predictable and capability is owned internally.
↑
Repeatability Across the Portfolio
When the same approach runs across business units or portcos, the evidence from the first run calibrates the next. The approach compounds in a way a single engagement cannot.

These are the numbers that appear in a diligence conversation about AI transformation. When they move, the story writes itself.

Evidence

Graded by Method, Not by Vendor

METR
Independent capability evaluations of frontier models on software engineering tasks. Pre-registered methodology, adversarially validated results. Highest methodological weight.
Weight
DORA
Annual State of DevOps Report. Multi-year longitudinal survey of delivery performance across thousands of teams. Broadly representative but self-selected sample.
Weight
GitClear
Analysis of 211 million lines of code change from 2019 to 2024, tracking churn, copy-paste rate and revert frequency. Longitudinal, code-level signal without self-report bias.
Weight
Adoption research
Independent studies of AI coding tool adoption at scale across GitHub Copilot, Cursor and Claude Code. Velocity gains are real; defect-rate outcomes depend on how the operating model is set up.
Weight
Vendor accounts
Self-reported outcomes from tool providers. Useful for directional signal; structurally incentivised toward positive framing. Treated as hypothesis, not evidence.
Weight

Our own approach would score a C on the same table: we have structured observation and pilot data, not a randomised controlled trial. We state this in advance rather than after the fact.

How We Engage

Twelve Weeks. Three Phases.

Phase One

Diagnose the Real Constraint

We start where the operating model breaks down — where decisions slow, where value disappears and where accountability is unclear. That diagnosis drives everything that follows. No tooling conversation until this is done.

Phase Two

Rebuild Around the Constraint

Product strategy, delivery model and measurement change together — not sequentially. Changes that cannot be connected to a measurable outcome are not made. We transfer capability, not dependency.

Phase Three

Prove It in the Operating Data

By the end of the engagement, the evidence lives in operating data — not in a presentation. Cost, speed and reliability are measurable. The story for a buyer is already forming.

Organisations We Have Worked With

Three Questions Worth Asking

  • What does our AI spend actually trace to in the operating numbers?
  • If a buyer asked us to demonstrate AI-native efficiency today, what would we show them?
  • Is our product designed around what AI now makes possible — or just augmented with it?

If you do not have fast answers to all three, that is the starting point.
